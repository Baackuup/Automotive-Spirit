The car in numbers

What 5,185 generations of cars launched since 1950 say, decade by decade: heavier, as long as in the 1950s, more and more SUVs, and electric.

Heavier and heavier

The median weight of a new generation went from 1,128 kg in the 1970s to 1,697 kg in the 2020s, +51%: safety structures, equipment, bigger cars and, lately, batteries.

As long as in the 1950s

American land yachts made the 1950s the longest decade; cars then shrank until the 1980s, and today's are back to the same median length.

The rise of the SUV

37% of the generations launched in the 2020s are SUVs or crossovers, against 2% in the 1970s.

The switch to electricity

Hybrid or fully electric: 58% of the generations launched in the 2020s have a traction battery or an electric motor, against 4% in the 2000s.

Method: road-car generations of the site's brands, by decade of launch (the current year is left out). Medians use the first length and weight given for each generation, usually the base version; values outside 2–7 m and 400–4,500 kg are left out. SUVs and crossovers are recognised by their class or body style. Point at a bar for the number of generations behind it.
