Driver assistance

What every new car in Europe must have

Since 7 July 2024, the General Safety Regulation makes a set of driver-assistance systems compulsory on every new vehicle sold in the EU.

The General Safety Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2019/2144, known as GSR2, sets safety requirements for the type approval of vehicles. It applied first to new vehicle types from July 2022, then to every new vehicle sold or registered from 7 July 2024: cars, vans, trucks and buses.

The systems now compulsory

Intelligent speed assistance (ISA): tells the driver the speed limit and warns when it is exceeded.

Automatic emergency braking (AEB): brakes on its own to avoid or reduce a collision with a vehicle ahead.

Emergency lane keeping (ELKS): steers back when the car is about to leave its lane.

Driver drowsiness and attention warning (DDAW): warns a driver who shows signs of tiredness.

Reversing detection by camera or sensors, an emergency stop signal (flashing brake lights under hard braking) and a standard way to fit an alcohol interlock.

What comes next

From July 2026, all new vehicles must also have advanced driver distraction warning (ADDW), which detects when the driver's eyes are not on the road, and automatic emergency braking extended to pedestrians and cyclists.
